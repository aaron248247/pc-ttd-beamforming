## 2. Analytical expectations (derived before the runs; see the methods document)

* **1A vacuum.** H = F(x+L)/F(x) = exp(+ikL) for MEEP's exp(-i omega t) convention, hence tau_g = +d(arg H)/d omega = L/c. The Yee grid adds
  tau_g = nL[1 + (omega dx)^2 (n^2 - S^2)/8] (S = dt/dx), positive, second order in dx.
* **1B dielectric.** tau_g = nL/c in a homogeneous medium, linear in L with zero intercept; the same Yee correction with n. A slab between vacuum monitor planes
  follows TMM including interface reflections (Fabry-Perot) and has excess delay tau_g - D/c.
* **1C Bragg stack.** TMM transmission, reflection, complex t and excess delay; stop-band edges tending to the infinite-lattice values f0[1 -+ (2/pi) asin((n_H-n_L)/(n_H+n_L))]
  as N grows; stop-band transmission decaying as exp(-2 acosh|cos K Lambda| N); T + R = 1.
* **1D 2-D crystal.** A y-uniform Hz plane wave on a mirror-symmetric crystal excites only y-even-Hz modes, so the stop band is the interval with no even-parity band at k_y = 0 (MPB), which can be
  wider than the omnidirectional gap. Finite-length effects: edges approach the band edges as N grows; Fabry-Perot fringes in the pass band; interface/termination resonances in the gap.

## 3. Results

### 1A vacuum (L = 10, analysis band 0.16-0.44)

* **The sign convention is established numerically, not assumed:** applying sign +1 to the FDTD transfer function gives a mean delay of 10.0022, sign -1 gives -10.0022; the project constant is `MEEP_SIGN = +1`.

## 5. Uncertainties (what each headline number carries)

* Vacuum / dielectric delays: the discretization error is positive, second order, and known in closed form; at res 40 it is 2.2e-3 a/c for L = 10 (0.02%). The extraction adds 1.5e-5. Termination floor ~1.5e-5.
* Slab/stack vs TMM: dominated by discretization, converging at order ~2; the pointwise delay error is largest at the sharpest resonances (max error 0.19 of a max TMM delay of 27.5 at res 160 for n = 3.46), so the median relative error and the fitted frequency scale are the meaningful summaries.
* **The delay estimator itself** has truncation error near resonances (1.2 for n = 3.46, df = 0.002; 6.3 for the Bragg stack at df = 0.0004). Whenever FDTD is compared with TMM, the same estimator is applied to both, so this does not enter the comparison; it does bound what delay *accuracy* a sampled estimator can deliver near sharp features, which matters in Phase 2.
* 2-D stop-band edges: see section 9 (components: discretization <= 1.0e-3, finite length / fringes / termination up to ~3e-3).

## 7. Bugs and unexpected numerical behavior found

1. **MEEP DFT frequency axis.** `add_dft_fields` takes (fcen, df, nfreq); I had passed (fmin, fmax, nfreq). The delay came out exactly 1.25x too large (the frequency-spacing ratio). Fixed by passing an explicit
   frequency list and verifying it against the DFT object; regression test added.
2. **Premature termination.** `stop_when_dft_decayed` stopped before the pulse reached a monitor 45 units away (L = 40 gave nonsense, flagged by |H| = 1.0 deviation). The driver now sets the minimum run time from the transit time; regression test added.
3. **Derivative-based delay estimator.** Its truncation error tau (d_omega tau)^2/6 is far larger than the unwrapping limit; replaced as the primary unwrapping-free method by the neighbor-ratio estimator and kept, with a test of its error law, as a cross-check.
4. **Aliasing cannot be detected from wrapped phase.** Documented as a test; the a-priori sampling guard is mandatory and enforced in the 1C script. The neighbor-ratio estimator has half the unwrapping delay limit.
5. **Cell rounding.** With an integer MEEP resolution the periodic height sqrt(3) a was rounded to 1.75 a (1% strain). The 2-D driver sets resolution = ny/sqrt(3); a test checks the allocated pixel count.
6. **Estimator comparison bias.** Comparing sampled FDTD delays with analytically differentiated TMM delays mixed estimator truncation into the "error" (a plateau independent of resolution). Fixed by applying the same estimator to both.
7. **Pointwise metrics at resonances are hypersensitive**: a resonance shifted by a discretization-sized amount produces a large local T or delay difference (max dT = 0.19 at res 160 for the 8-period stack). Replaced by a fitted frequency scale s (effective optical-thickness error), band-edge positions, rms and median errors, with the pointwise maxima still reported.
8. **R + T = 1 is dominated by ring-down termination, not by physics**: 1e-2 at decay tolerance 1e-8 and 5.6e-7 at 1e-14 for the 8-period stack. In 2-D, a single band-edge resonance (f ~ 0.184-0.186) keeps R + T - 1 near 1e-2 even after 3e4 time units, while the median over the band is ~5e-5. That is a high-Q finite resonator not resolved by the frequency sampling, not an error.
9. **Slope-of-ln-T edge estimator failed in 2-D.** Validated on TMM (recovers the infinite-lattice edges to 1.4e-4 to 3.9e-4 for N = 6-20), but in the 2-D gap pointwise T is not monotonic in N: a termination-induced
   resonance near f = 0.244 (T = 6e-3, 1.6e-3, 2.1e-5, 1.3e-6 for N = 6, 8, 10, 12) and a numerical floor (~1e-8, set by the decay tolerance) make per-frequency slopes meaningless. The estimator is retained, tested, and *not used for the 2-D result*. The
   termination study (faces moved) confirmed the resonance is a surface/termination effect: it moves to 0.224 / 0.216 / 0.274 for other face positions and its amplitude is below 6.4e-6 at N = 12-16 for all four terminations.
10. Test-authoring errors corrected from derivation, not loosened: an ill-posed unwrapping-boundary test (float equality at the limit), a test expecting the wrapped-step check to catch aliasing (it cannot), a ratio-method test above its two-sample limit, a tolerance
    taken from the wrong resolution (res 40 value used for a res 20 test), and one `or`-based vacuous assertion replaced by exact expected outcomes.

## 8. MEEP vs TMM (1-D Bragg stack, quarter-wave n = 1 / sqrt 12, period 1)

Analytic infinite-lattice stop band: 0.2022-0.4421 (f0 = 0.3222). MEEP and TMM agree through the whole transfer function; see the figure `p1_bragg.png` and appendix T1C.

* **Convergence (N = 8).** The best-fit frequency scale s (effective optical-thickness error) is 5.2e-4 at res 80, 1.3e-4 at res 160, 2.7e-5 at res 320 (observed order 1.94). Upper -3 dB edge error: -2.8e-4, -6.2e-5, -1.3e-5 (order 1.93). Median relative error in complex t: 2.6e-2, 6.3e-3, 1.4e-3 at res 80, 160, 320. Median excess-delay error: 0.13, 0.031, 0.008 (TMM delays reach 147 at the band-edge resonances; the max pointwise delay error is 24, 5.8, 1.2 at those resonances).
* **Stop-band edges vs N** (res 80): MEEP and TMM −3 dB edges differ by at most 1.4e-5 (lower) and 3.6e-4 (upper) for every N = 2-12, and both approach the infinite-lattice edges monotonically (N = 12: 0.1999 / 0.4441 MEEP, 0.1999 / 0.4444 TMM).
* **Energy.** R + T - 1 is 1.4e-4 at the base setting (decay tolerance 1e-12) and 5.6e-7 at 1e-14.
* **Stop-band decay.** MEEP's decay per period of ln T versus the analytic Bloch rate: 1.139 vs 1.117 (f = 0.215, +2.0%), 1.594 vs 1.591 (0.23, +0.2%), 1.983 vs 1.986 (0.25, -0.14%).
* **Delay in the periodic medium.** With the same estimator applied to both and only where T > 1e-3, the median relative excess-delay difference (|Δτ| / max(|τ_TMM|, 1)) is 0.42% for N = 8 at res 160 (median |τ_TMM| = 8.6, max 141), 0.44% for N = 4 at res 80 (max 21.7), and 2.5% for N = 12 at res 80, where band-edge resonances reach 349 and the pointwise maximum difference is 119. Longer stacks have sharper resonances and need finer grids for the same delay accuracy; the band-edge resonance peaks are reproduced (figure `p1_bragg.png`).
* PML, source coverage, decay and placement effects are listed in section 4; none exceeds the discretization error except a coverage below ~1e-4 or a decay tolerance above 1e-10.

## 9. MEEP finite 2-D crystal vs MPB

**MPB prediction (k_y = 0 rectangular cell, r/a = 0.30, res 32/64/128):** y-even-Hz gap [0.20705, 0.2890] at res 128 (converged to 5e-5 against res 64). The all-parity gap is [0.20705, 0.2744] and coincides with the bulk omnidirectional gap
[0.20706, 0.27442]; the odd-parity band occupying 0.2744-0.289 cannot couple to a mirror-symmetric plane wave.

**FDTD stop band** (T_max over lengths N, contiguous interval around mid-gap with T_max < 1e-2; long runs with minimum run time 20000):

* At the finest resolution (ny = 48) the interval is [0.20845, 0.28916] for N = 8, 10, 12 (and [0.21023, 0.28884] when N = 6 is included): the **upper edge agrees with MPB to 1.6e-4** (0.06%); the lower edge lies **inside** the MPB gap by 1.4e-3 (0.7%) for N = 8-12.
* **Symmetry test passed:** in the window 0.2754-0.288 (above the all-parity gap top, below the even gap top) T is below 2.7e-3 for N = 6, 1.5e-4 for N = 8 and 5e-5 (N = 12) to 2e-5 (N = 16) at ny = 48. The stop band does extend to the parity-resolved gap top, as MPB predicts, not just to 0.2744.
* **Deep-gap attenuation** is exponential in N and resolution-independent: 1.45 per column (1.437-1.462 over ny = 24-48; values with T > 3e-7 only; N = 6, 8, 10).

**Uncertainty budget for the edges (at T < 1e-2):**

| Source | Size | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| MPB itself (res 64 -> 128) | 5e-5 | `1d_mpb_directional.json` |
| FDTD discretization (ny 24 -> 48) | <= 1.0e-3 (single-length edges) | appendix T1D, "Contributions" |
| Finite length and fringes (single N = 8-16, ny = 48) | lower edge in [-2.7e-4, +1.4e-3], upper in [+4.8e-4, +2.7e-3] of the MPB edge | appendix T1D |
| Termination (faces moved by -0.15 ... +0.30), N = 12-16 | lower edge in [0.20635, 0.21020], upper in [0.28758, 0.28936] | appendix T1D, termination table |

Combined, every variant studied (resolution, N = 6-16, thresholds 1e-2 and 1e-3, and four terminations at N = 12-16) places the FDTD stop-band edges within 3.5e-3 of the MPB parity-resolved edges (at most 1.7% of the lower-edge frequency and 0.9% of the upper); the lower edge is typically inside the MPB gap by 1e-3 to 3e-3 (the expected side), and the upper edge lies between 1.4e-3 below and 2.7e-3 above the MPB edge: a finite section at a finite attenuation threshold behaves as if its gap were slightly narrower at the bottom, and low coupling into the flat even band just above 0.289 keeps T below 1e-2 for a few 1e-3 into the band. That last statement is an
interpretation consistent with the termination and length dependence, not an independently proven mechanism.

**Finite-length and boundary effects, as observed:** (i) edges converge toward the band edges only slowly and non-monotonically in N because the transmission near an edge is controlled by band-edge group velocity and interface coupling; (ii) pass-band Fabry-Perot fringes
(T between ~0.3 and ~1) and, at f ~ 0.184-0.186, a resonance whose ring-down exceeds 3e4 time units; (iii) a termination-dependent in-gap resonance (surface-state-like) whose amplitude falls with N and whose frequency moves with the face position; (iv) a
numerical floor ~1e-8 from the decay tolerance below which T is meaningless.

## 10. Limitations

* 1-D tests use point monitors in a 1-D cell; the 2-D test uses a periodic cell with a y-uniform source, i.e. normal incidence only. Nothing here exercises mode decomposition, finite-width waveguides, or the W1 line defect.
* Edge comparison is at fixed T thresholds of finite sections; it cannot resolve the MPB edges better than about 1e-3 to 3.5e-3, and the MPB eigenfrequencies themselves carry 5e-5.
* The complex-wavevector (evanescent) decay in the 2-D gap is measured but has **no independent MPB/analytic counterpart** here; it is validated only through the 1-D TMM/Bloch analogue.
* 2-D ring-down limits: T below ~1e-7 is numerical floor at decay tolerance 1e-8; band-edge resonances with Q beyond ~1e4 are unresolved at df = 0.002 and not converged at 3e4 time units.
* Lossless, non-dispersive materials; 2-D (no out-of-plane radiation); TE only; r/a = 0.30 only; ny <= 64 (res <= 37 px/a).
* The estimators' truncation error near resonances (section 5) will matter for Phase 2 group-delay extraction at high n_g and must be handled by frequency spacing, not post hoc.

**Gate.** Phase 1 passes if the following statement is supported:

> The FDTD transmission/phase/group-delay extraction pipeline reproduces known propagation delays, agrees with an independent transfer-matrix solution for a periodic dielectric benchmark, and gives a finite 2-D photonic-crystal stop band consistent with the independently computed MPB band structure within documented numerical uncertainty.

* *Known propagation delays:* yes. Vacuum and homogeneous-dielectric delays equal L/c and nL/c up to the closed-form Yee-grid correction, which the extraction reproduces to 1.5e-5 (vacuum) and 4e-6 (n = 2); delay is linear in L with intercepts below 3.4e-6; the sign convention is verified numerically. Errors converge at measured order 2.0.
* *Periodic dielectric vs TMM:* yes, with the stated meaning of agreement: the transfer function converges to TMM at measured order ~2 (median relative error in t 1.4e-3 at res 320), stop-band edges agree to 3e-4 (res 80), Bloch decay to 2%, 0.2%, 0.14%, energy to 1e-6 at tight termination. Pointwise delay agreement at the sharpest band-edge resonances needs res >= 320 to reach below 1% of the peak delay, and is reported as such.
* *2-D crystal vs MPB:* yes within the documented budget: edges within 3.5e-3 of MPB in every variant studied (<= 1.7% of the edge frequency) with the lower edge typically on the expected (inside-the-gap) side, the parity-resolved gap top reproduced (symmetry test), deep-gap attenuation resolution-independent. The comparison is *not* an identity, and the finite-length/termination components are documented rather than eliminated.

**The gate is judged passed**, with the caveats of section 10. Items I would like reviewed before Phase 2: (1) the edge-agreement tolerance I am treating as acceptable (<= 3.5e-3, <= 1.7% of the edge frequency, dominated by finite-length/termination effects, not discretization; discretization contributes <= 1.0e-3); (2) that the Phase 2 transfer-function work
must use mode decomposition rather than the plane-wave flux used here; (3) the frequency-sampling and estimator-truncation requirement for group delay near high-n_g features.
